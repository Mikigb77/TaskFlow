**TaskFlow: critique evaluation and proposed improvement backlog**

Reviewed 22 September 2026 against the current dev checkout, app version 1.0.0+4002, based on commit 50afe7a. The existing uncommitted build-tool changes do not affect the product findings below. This is a proposal, not an implementation or a commitment to build every feature.

Source: [the supplied critique](C:/Users/mikig/Desktop/Custom/MikiHacks/TaskFlow/CRITIQUE.md). I checked the relevant Flutter screens, models, persistence/sync code, notification scheduler, Django extraction code, existing tests, and the separate website's source. This was a source review; I did not conduct a new phone usability study, latency benchmark, or notification delivery test. Existing test cases support particular behaviors, not an overall claim of production reliability.

**Overall assessment**

The critique is strongest on capture recovery, reminder behavior, and unnecessary interaction work. Its central diagnosis is useful: the app has promising engineering foundations, but turning a thought into a dependable, useful task still asks too much of the user. Those defects deserve attention before another broad feature expansion.

Its prioritization and proposed implementations need revision. It mixes confirmed defects, usability hypotheses, missing features, and major product choices. Several precise-sounding claims about speed, abandonment, competitor limitations, and implementation effort are unsupported. Building everything in the document would also work against the simple app we want.

My recommended product direction is fast capture by voice or typing, easy correction, and dependable follow-through. Preserve the existing visual identity and Calendar as the first screen. Improve the agenda's prominence within Calendar. Build checklists and basic lists after the capture/reminder foundations, then validate demand for collaboration and advanced planning.

**Where I would revise the critique**

1. **The speed baseline is not established.** From an already-open app with permissions granted, the main microphone automatically starts recording. The normal uncomplicated route is microphone, stop, save: three deliberate button actions. The critique's five-action count overstates this route. Both review and manual editor have fixed bottom save buttons, so scrolling is not always required to save. Cognitive load and waiting remain real concerns. Measure cold launch separately and measure processing after speech ends; a 40-second spoken list cannot become a saved task in five seconds from its beginning.

2. **On-device speech is a promising optimization, with a fallback requirement.** Android's generic SpeechRecognizer can use remote servers; explicit on-device recognition requires an available service and language support. Apple's recognizer also exposes a capability check. A native speech plugin alone does not guarantee offline operation or partial results on every device. Local transcription still leaves structured AI extraction online unless we add local parsing or retain a pending capture. See [Android speech documentation](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/speech/SpeechRecognizer) and [Apple's capability documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechrecognizer/supportsondevicerecognition).

3. **No clarification questions is not evidence that a draft is correct.** The extraction schema has no calibrated confidence measure, and even such a measure would need evaluation. Start with a compact, one-tap confirmation and direct correction. Consider optional auto-save only after measuring errors, with visible results, durable Undo, and clear user expectations. A confidently wrong appointment is a worse result than one extra confirmation.

4. **Repeating the current summary is not a complete fix.** Its body is a snapshot of three tasks when scheduling runs. Simply changing the repeat flag would replay stale dates and completed items. The current one-shot design deliberately avoids that, as the backend README explains. A persistent daily check-in can use generic wording; a task-specific digest needs fresh content and a tested scheduling strategy. Currently up to two one-shot summaries are scheduled, not literally one notification total.

5. **Exact reminders need capability handling and delivery testing.** Replacing one scheduling enum is insufficient. Permission, revocation, reboot, timezone changes, and supported device behavior matter. Expose whether an alert is scheduled, disabled, or approximate; do not label an OS scheduling acceptance as proof of delivery. See [Android's alarm guidance](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms).

6. **Automatic stopping and local date parsing need bounded designs.** A fixed short silence threshold can cut off someone thinking through several tasks. Hold-to-talk is useful as an option, but should not be the only accessible control. Likewise, a local parser is not incapable of failure: ambiguous times, regional dates, literal titles, corrections, and language differences all matter. Offer recognized schedule chips that can be accepted or dismissed and leave unsupported text intact.

7. **Some website accusations overreach.** The [FAQ](C:/Users/mikig/Desktop/Custom/MikiHacks/TaskFlow/TaskFlowWeb/src/app/page.tsx:14) already explains online AI and local attachments. The [demo](C:/Users/mikig/Desktop/Custom/MikiHacks/TaskFlow/TaskFlowWeb/src/components/TaskDemo.tsx:162) explicitly describes sample examples with no microphone or AI request. Artificial delay would not make it more accurate. The signed-in-account requirement is genuinely outdated in two FAQ answers and should be corrected. Attachment limitations should also appear at attachment/account-restoration decision points. The phrase about the shape of a day is vague positioning, not an explicit promise of an hourly timeline; clarify it before using it to justify a large new feature.

8. **The competitive argument is too absolute in both directions.** Basic assistant capture is a serious benchmark, but it is not universally configured or available as described. Google's current documentation also includes conversational task creation and list-based inputs, so the claim that assistants only create one simple reminder is too narrow. Structured AI input alone is not a proven differentiator. Test whether people prefer TaskFlow's complete capture, organization, and follow-through experience. See [Gemini's supported task workflows](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15230285?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid).

9. **Missing features are not all defects.** A flat list can be an intentional simple product. Shared lists, tags, standalone notes, external calendars, and time blocking serve different audiences. The critique establishes their absence, not their usage frequency or value to our intended users. Checklists are especially promising because they directly improve the kind of speech capture already supported, but the current AI schema does not yet produce nested checklists; extraction must evolve alongside storage and UI.

10. **Existing preferences should guide design changes.** Calendar-first and an accessible light/dark button were explicit earlier choices. Keep Calendar as the landing screen and make its agenda more immediate. Experiment with a less repetitive theme control instead of removing a requested feature wholesale. Keep confirmation for deleting a whole recurring series until reliable restoration exists; completion Undo alone does not make deletion reversible.

**Implementation details that materially change the order**

Durable capture is more than retaining a temporary WAV. Store a capture ID, owner/guest scope, original capture time and timezone, input, processing state, and remaining drafts in app-private durable storage. Pin relative expressions such as tomorrow to the original capture time even if Retry happens the next day. Checkpoint usable partial content and recover after process death where the platform permits; test what survives instead of promising zero loss under every hardware failure. Stop recording on interruption and keep what was captured; do not continue recording covertly in the background. Remove retained raw input after a successful durable save or explicit discard, and explain retention in the existing privacy copy. User-initiated discard remains intentional deletion.

Partial batch saving depends on that capture store. Mark each saved draft with its task identity transactionally so retry cannot create duplicates or erase the unresolved remainder. An Edit callback currently confirms the whole draft; replacing it with field-level clarification should not mark an unrelated ambiguity resolved just because the title changed. Compact review can ship before any automatic saving experiment.

Reminder configuration and reminder delivery are separate jobs. Keep priority as importance, and reminders as alerts. Offer a sensible editable default for timed tasks, an explicit off option, and a separate choice for date-only tasks. Preserve snooze by task/occurrence identity through ordinary edits and account changes. The current scheduler cancels all alarms before rebuilding, swallows exceptions, and bounds recurring projections to 60 days and 50 advance alerts. A replacement needs stable identifiers, observable failures and tested replenishment. Define the supported behavior under notification denial, exact-alarm access changes and device restrictions; background jobs should not be presented as a guarantee of precise timing.

Changing speech recognition must preserve capture recovery. Verify how the chosen native bridge handles partial transcripts, interrupted recordings, audio ownership and long pauses before committing to it. Use the existing bounded Django extraction endpoint for recognized text; no new agent framework is needed. The first implementation target is Android, with English and Spanish as useful initial evaluation candidates. Confirm the actual supported language/device matrix before advertising offline voice.

New checklists, lists, reminder settings and recurrence exceptions must extend the existing SQLite/Django API and conflict handling. Do not replace the sync architecture just to add those fields. Start with one checklist level and simple lists; defer nested projects, dependencies and broad collaboration until usage warrants them.

**What success should mean**

These are proposed acceptance checks and experiment targets, not measurements of today's app or public performance promises.

| Area | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|
| Capture retention | No discarded successfully retained input in the test matrix: airplane mode, provider 503, timeout, incoming-call interruption, backgrounding, navigation away, process restart and account change. Repeated Retry/Save produces one set of tasks. |
| Capture speed | Measure cold-launch-to-ready, time speaking, stop-to-usable-draft and confirmation-to-local-save separately. Report p50/p95 plus failures and cancellations by input mode, language and connection. Aim initially for median stop-to-draft below five seconds on the chosen baseline device/network, then revise from evidence. |
| Correctness | Use a repeatable set of short tasks, multi-task speech, checklists, missing dates, date-only tasks, ambiguous hours, corrections, unsupported repeats and daylight-saving boundaries. Score omitted/extra tasks and wrong schedule fields, not just valid JSON. |
| Interaction | Observe a small exploratory group, for example 5–8 people, creating milk, tomorrow's errand, an appointment with preparation notes, and several tasks in one capture. Compare with their existing method. Treat that as formative evidence, not a statistically representative retention study. |
| Reminders | On physical target devices, test the app closed, rebooted, with notifications disabled/re-enabled, timezone/DST changes, edited/completed tasks, recurrence, snooze and more tasks than the scheduling window. Inspect both requested alarms and actual receipt. |
| Simplicity and access | Small screens, enlarged text, screen readers and both themes retain labeled controls, a usable typed path, and manual recording control. More capabilities must not require more fields for a plain task. |
| Product value | Track successful first capture, correction rate, repeat voluntary use and why testers switch back to another tool. A voice-usage percentage alone does not prove that the product helps. Keep diagnostics free of raw audio, task titles and transcripts. |

**Recommended first implementation batch**

Begin with B01 capture recovery, B02 explicit reminder controls, the B03 delivery design, and B04 minimal measurement. B05 copy corrections are small enough to accompany that work in the separate web repo. Then ship B06, B08 and B09 to shorten capture and correction, while B07 resolves native speech feasibility. Apply the small Calendar/navigation improvements without replacing the landing screen. Add B14 checklists once this loop is dependable.

This sequence addresses the critique's strongest evidence and creates measurements for the larger choices. Sharing, a complete notes product and advanced calendar planning remain proposals to validate rather than prerequisites for a useful first release.
